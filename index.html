01 /Finding the business

First, find a business worth building.

Most of our work happens before the first product is built. Our agents investigate markets, challenge ideas, and work out whether a stranger would choose what we could make. Finding a convincing answer is the hardest part.

  1. Look for what has become possible.

    We study both what people already buy and what better AI makes possible for the first time. New capabilities can change what is affordable, who can be served, and what people might want once they see it.

  2. Make the case from the customer’s side.

    An idea has to survive its alternatives: established products, specialists, doing nothing, and the customer’s own AI. We investigate the reason to buy, the route to a first purchase, and the work and cost behind the complete result.

  3. Challenge the answer.

    Agents develop and challenge competing cases, check the underlying evidence, and look for what would change the decision. We revisit the assumptions as models, markets and customer behavior change. A working demo answers only a small part of the question.

Our aim is to make finding the next worthwhile business a repeatable capability of the company.

The larger idea

How much of a company can AI take on?

Choosing the opportunity is the beginning. We want AI to carry it through product development, customer acquisition, delivery, support, and the decisions about what to improve next.

That changes the economics of starting a business. An opportunity can become worth pursuing when the cost of skilled work falls, or when a small team can deliver something that once required an entire organization.

We’re building toward a company that can keep discovering and operating new businesses as AI’s capabilities expand. Its success has to show up in useful products, satisfied customers, and sound economics.

Agents work on research, engineering, testing, and deployment. Customer feedback and product use help determine the next changes, connecting development to the operation it serves.

Running the business

Support, account administration, analytics, and supplier costs are part of the work. Each reveals practical limits and opportunities that a prototype alone would never show.
